1. The business process: ticket booking, payment and gate admission

Purpose. This process covers what happens from the moment an event is put on sale until a ticket holder walks through the gate: a customer chooses seats, the seats are held, the customer pays, the booking is confirmed and the ticket is scanned once at the entrance. It is the core process of SmartTicket because every other function (reports, audit, the dashboard) depends on the records it creates.

Objectives. (1) Sell each seat to only one buyer. (2) Stop bulk buying by limiting a customer to 6 tickets per event. (3) Free seats that are held but not paid within 15 minutes. (4) Accept a ticket at the gate only once, and only for the right event. (5) Record every action, including refused ones, so organisers can see what happened.

Expected outcome. A paid booking with seats marked SOLD, a single ADMITTED scan per ticket on event day, and a complete audit trail. Refused requests end cleanly with a logged reason instead of a corrupted record.

Problem addressed. Ticketing in Kigali suffers from scalpers buying many cheap seats to resell, seats sold twice, and one screenshot of a QR code being used by several people. Organisers usually see only total sales, not the attempts that were blocked.

Why it matters. Ticket sales are the organiser's revenue, and a double sale or a fake entry costs money and trust and can make the venue unsafe if it is over capacity. Because the rules are written as PL/SQL in the database, the rules apply in the same way whichever app or counter sells the ticket.

Delete / archive. No activity physically deletes a record. A cancelled or expired booking, a failed payment and a refused scan are kept and only change status, so the history stays complete for audit and reporting. AUDIT_LOG is append-only. Tasks 3, 8, 9, 15, 16, 20, 21 and 23 are actions by people or the payment provider that pass information on but do not write to the database themselves.

5. Process explanation

How the process begins. The process starts when an event is approved for sale. The Event Organiser publishes it (task 1): the database stores the event, a price for each tier (VIP, Premium, Silver, Bronze) and one ticket row per seat, all AVAILABLE. From then on a customer can browse open events and see how many seats are free in each tier (task 2).

Booking. The customer selects up to six seats (task 3) and sends the event, the seat list and their customer code to SmartTicket. The procedure sp_book_tickets checks the request (task 4) and takes two decisions. First, is the event open, i.e. scheduled and inside its sales window? If not, the request is refused, an ORA-20003 entry is written to the audit log and the process ends. Second, are all the seats still free and would the customer stay within 6 tickets for this event? If not, the attempt is logged (ORA-20001 or ORA-20002) and the customer is sent back to choose other seats. When both checks pass, the seats become HELD and a PENDING booking is created with a 15-minute hold (task 7).

Payment. The customer pays with MTN MoMo or a card (task 8). The payment provider processes it and returns a reference and a result (task 9). sp_record_payment stores the payment and compares the amount with fn_booking_total (task 10). If the payment failed or the amount is wrong, it is marked FAILED and logged (task 11), and the customer decides whether to try again while the hold is still valid. If it succeeds, the payment is COMPLETED, the booking CONFIRMED and the seats SOLD (task 12), and the customer receives an e-ticket whose QR code carries the ticket id (task 14). If 15 minutes pass without payment, the timer on task 8 ends the customer's path; a scheduler job that runs every minute (task 13) marks the booking EXPIRED and returns the seats to sale.

Gate admission. On event day the customer shows the QR code (task 15) and gate staff scan it (task 16). The ticket id and gate code go to sp_scan_ticket (task 17), which decides whether the ticket is SOLD, belongs to this event and has not been used. If yes, an ADMITTED scan is recorded, the ticket becomes USED (task 18) and staff let the person in (task 20). If no, a DENIED scan with its reason is recorded (task 19) and staff refuse entry (task 21). A copied QR code therefore works only once.

How information moves. Information flows from people to the database as requests (seat list, payment reference, ticket id) and back as decisions (accepted, refused with a reason). Every change goes through PL/SQL, so no actor writes to a table directly, and each step leaves a status that the next step reads: AVAILABLE → HELD → SOLD → USED.

Support for operations and management. For daily operations, the process prevents double sales, limits scalping, frees unpaid seats and stops repeated entry without depending on staff judgement. For management, the organiser watches a live Power BI dashboard (task 22) that reads sales, occupancy and denied attempts directly from the same tables. When the data shows an event close to selling out, a spike in denied scans or a slow gate, the organiser decides what to do (task 23), for example opening another gate, releasing a held-back section or flagging a suspicious buyer.
